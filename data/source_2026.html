Bhadra — verified against Swiss Ephemeris

↑ Back to Table of Contents
Method. Every window below was recomputed with the Swiss Ephemeris, not copied. A Bhadra (Vishti karana) is a 6° step of Moon−Sun elongation, so the eight windows of each lunar month fall on karana numbers 8, 15, 22, 29, 36, 43, 50 and 57. Boundaries were found by bisection on the exact 6° multiples; all times are IST.

Day / Night Bhadra is read from karana parity — odd number (first half of the tithi) = Day Bhadra, even number (second half) = Night Bhadra. It is a property of the tithi-half and has nothing to do with daylight, so it never changes with timezone.

Sign is the Moon’s sidereal longitude (Lahiri) at the moment the window opens; Bhadra Nature 2 follows the classical loka rule — Aries/Taurus/Gemini/Scorpio → Swarg, Virgo/Libra/Sagittarius/Capricorn → Patal, Cancer/Leo/Aquarius/Pisces → Earth (Mrityu-loka), the malefic case.

Bhadra Nature 1 asks where the window opens: a Bhadra beginning after sunrise is “in Day Time,” one beginning after sunset is “in Night Time.” Sunrise and sunset are computed for 18°N58′, 72°E50′ — Mumbai, the reference place declared by this calendar. Result 1 then applies the crossing rule: a Bhadra falling in the opposite period is defused (Positive), the same period is live (Negative). So Day Bhadra opening after sunset is Positive, Day Bhadra opening after sunrise is Negative, and the mirror for Night Bhadra. Overall is Positive when both axes are Positive, Negative when both are Negative, Neutral when they split.
