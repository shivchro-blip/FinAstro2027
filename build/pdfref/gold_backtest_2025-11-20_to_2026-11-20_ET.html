How each occurrence was scored

  • Entry = the last daily close before the event (events at or after 17:00 ET count from the next session; panchang day-rules from that day's open).
  • Windows of 3 days or more (a planet in a nakshatra or sign, Sun contacts…) are scored from entry to the last close inside the window, clipped to the backtest period. Instants and short windows (new moon, darshan, rise/set, ingress, vedha starts) are scored on the 5-trading-day move; 1-, 3- and 10-day moves are shown as a sensitivity check.
  • Moves under ±0.5% are FLAT. Rules whose verdict is Mixed or Neutral (both vedha kinds at once; the Indian festival-demand dates) are listed but not scored.
  • Hit rate = worked ÷ (worked + failed); flat occurrences are left out of both.
  • Base rate = the hit rate a rule would get by chance: among all windows of the same length in the same backtest year that moved more than 0.5%, the share that moved the rule's way. Over the whole period a 5-day window that moved went up 50% of the time, so a bullish rule needs to beat 50% and a bearish rule 50% to show any edge. Using each year's own base rate means a bullish rule gets no credit just for firing in a rising year.
  • Rule verdict: Worked = hit rate at least 10 points above its base rate and the average move went the stated way; Failed = at least 10 points below and the average move went the wrong way; otherwise Mixed. Most rules fire only 1–6 times a year, so one year is not enough to be statistically sure of any single rule: read these numbers as evidence, not proof.
