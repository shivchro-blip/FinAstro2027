Lesson 9.2 Core Concepts — The Lunar Nodes and Their Cycle

Astronomical The lunar nodes are the two points where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic (the Sun's apparent path): the North (ascending) Node — Rahu, and the South (descending) Node — Ketu, always exactly opposite. They are not physical bodies but moving intersection points, and they regress — move backward through the zodiac — always retrograde.

Astronomical The line of nodes completes one full backward revolution — the draconic or nodal period — in 18.61 years (about 6,798 days), which works out to roughly 1.55 years (566 days) per sign. (Confirmed by computation: 18.613 yr, ~1.551 yr / 567 days per sign.) Two refinements:

  • True vs. mean node. The mean node is a smoothed average; the true (osculating) node wobbles slightly around it. They differ by up to ~1.5°, which matters at sign boundaries (§9.6). McWhirter-lineage work generally uses the true node.
  • Why the nodes matter beyond McWhirter. Eclipses occur only near the nodes — the hinge to Chapter 10 — and the same nodal regression drives the ~18.6-year lunar standstill / declination cycle, a genuine geophysical rhythm that modulates tides (and, some argue, climate). The 18.6-year beat is therefore a real astronomical cycle, independent of any market claim.
Callout — Regression, Not Progression Unlike the planets, the nodes move backward through the signs. So McWhirter's curve runs Aquarius → Capricorn → Sagittarius → … → Leo → … → Aquarius — the node entering each earlier sign in turn. Keep the direction straight or the whole curve inverts.

Lesson 9.3 Historical Framework — The McWhirter Theory

Historical Louise McWhirter's 1937 Astrology and Stock Market Forecasting was the first coherent, testable system correlating a planetary cycle with business activity. Her central claim: the primary trend of business volume, finance, and stock prices is delineated by the North Node's 18.6-year transit through the signs, tracing a repeating "normal curve of business activity." Historical Note the geometry: Aquarius (low) and Leo (high) are opposite signs, half the 18.6-year cycle (~9.3 years) apart — a clean, symmetric model. McWhirter's headline historical anchors:

  • She identified the summer-1933 Depression low with the North Node in Aquarius. (Verified: true node ~29.9° Aquarius, summer 1933.)
  • She predicted the next peak for November 1942 with the North Node in Leo — a call that coincided with the massive WWII-buildup stimulus. (Verified: the node was at the Leo/Virgo cusp by Nov 1942, having transited Leo just prior.)

Historical Two side-notes. First, McWhirter also offered a short-term timing method based on the Moon transiting sensitive points of the NYSE chart and the planets she took to "rule" it — material we defer to Chapters 13–14. Second, a persistent curiosity: because no other works or biographical traces of "Louise McWhirter" are known, some analysts (e.g., Bucholtz) speculate the name was a pseudonym for W. D. Gann. This is unverified lore, flagged as such — interesting, not established.

Lesson 9.4 Market Correlation Analysis — The Honest Test, and Today's Position

Why the theory is attractive

Framework McWhirter's model has real virtues: it is a single, dated, falsifiable curve (not a vague "the planets influence markets"); its ~18.6-year length closely matches the independently-documented ~18-year real-estate/land cycle (Chapter 8), a convergence proponents find compelling; and it gives a clear primary-trend context — are we near the Aquarius low or the Leo high?

The honest evidence — including the proponents' own admission

Observed The candor this book demands is unusually easy to source here, because the theory's own supporters concede it often fails. Analysts who chart the S&P 500 against the node state plainly that the basic theory was not consistently reflected in actual market movements, and they reach for "secondary factors" — other cycles and central-bank intervention — said to modify outcomes by a fifth or more. When a model needs that many escape hatches to explain its misses, the core signal is weak. Four hard cautions:

  • The ~18–19-year cycle zoo. Several different cycles cluster near this length — the Saros (18.03-yr eclipse cycle), the Metonic (19-yr), the lunar declination (18.6-yr), the nodal regression (18.6-yr), plus the variable solar cycle. They overlap, and it is dangerously easy to conflate or cherry-pick among them to fit any past turn.
  • The independent real-estate cycle confound. The ~18-year property cycle has non-astrological drivers (credit, land, demographics). A turn near a nodal phase may be obeying the economic cycle, not the node.
  • Small N (the Chapter 8 ceiling, again). Reliable market history holds only about five or six complete 18.6-year nodal cycles — far too few to validate a twelve-phase curve. The theory is structurally unprovable from the data.
  • Loose "fits" and outright errors in the literature. Proponents' fit-claims are often slack — and at least one widely-circulated write-up mis-dates the February-2020 (COVID) top as the "dot-com bubble" top (which was 2000). If the supporting material confuses two famous tops twenty years apart, treat its "the node predicted this" claims with great caution.

Lesson 9.6 Edge Cases and Cautions
  1. Don't conflate the ~18–19-year cycles. Astronomical (caution) Nodal regression (18.61 yr), the Saros (18.03 yr), the Metonic (19 yr), and lunar declination (18.6 yr) are distinct cycles of similar length. Mixing them — or switching among them to fit a chart — is a primary route to self-deception here.
  2. True vs. mean node shifts the timing. The Aquarius ingress is late July 2026 (true node) but is often cited ~October 2026 (mean/smoothed). Near a sign boundary the node's wobble matters; state which node you use and treat the ingress as a window, not a day.
  3. The literature's fits are loose — and sometimes wrong. Observed Proponents' "the node called this turn" claims are frequently post-hoc, and at least one popular source mis-labels the 2020 COVID top as the dot-com top. Audit any fit-claim against actual dated history before believing it.
  4. Small N and the independent real-estate cycle. Observed ~5–6 cycles cannot validate the curve, and the genuine ~18-year property cycle (credit/land-driven) may be the real signal masquerading as the node. Don't grant the planets credit the economy may be owed.
  5. The "secondary factors" escape hatch cuts both ways. Proponents excuse misses via "other cycles + central banks, up to ~20%." Fair as far as it goes — but a model that needs large discretionary adjustments to fit is, by that admission, not a precise forecasting tool. Treat its outputs as soft context.
  6. The standing evidence caveat. Observed The nodal/McWhirter cycle has no validated predictive power over markets. Its honest role is a falsifiable context for the primary trend and a pointer to the eclipse windows — confirmed only by price, governed by risk.
