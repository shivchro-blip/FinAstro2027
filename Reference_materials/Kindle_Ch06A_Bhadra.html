The Celestial Ledger · Volume I · Chapter Six-A

Bhadra — The Shadow on the Clock

The Vishti karana: the one element of the Panchanga that Indian practice treats not as a flavour but as an outright prohibition on beginning anything. What it is, how to compute it to the minute, why its day/night label has nothing to do with daylight — and what a market practitioner should and should not do with it.
◑ ✦ ◐

Chapter 6 named the five limbs of the Panchanga and developed four of them. Tithi, vara, nakshatra and yoga each got their paragraph. The fifth — karana — was defined in six words as "a half-tithi" and left there. This chapter pays that debt, because the karana carries something the other four limbs do not: a rule that tells you to stop.

Every other element of the almanac tints a day. It says this session may feel optimistic, that one heavy, this one nervous. Bhadra says something categorically different. It says: not now — do not begin. It is the closest thing in the Indian toolkit to a hard veto, and it is therefore the element most likely to be misused by a trader who mistakes a prohibition on starting for a prediction about direction.

The claim-type key — the five labels, unchanged The honest labels carry forward from Chapter 6 without addition. Classical an established rule of the tradition · Astronomical a verifiable astronomical fact · Observed an empirical finding or caveat · Framework this book's market interpretation · Verify to confirm. This chapter leans unusually hard on Astronomical, because Bhadra is arithmetic before it is doctrine — its timing is not a matter of interpretation at all. Every window quoted here was computed with Swiss Ephemeris for calendar year 2026; none was copied from a published almanac, for reasons Lesson 6A.6 makes uncomfortably clear.
A name collision, cleared up first Bhadra in this chapter is the Vishti karana. It has nothing whatever to do with Purva Bhadrapada and Uttara Bhadrapada, the twenty-fifth and twenty-sixth nakshatras met in Chapter 0. The shared syllable is coincidental to our purposes. A reader searching this book for "Bhadra" will land on both; they are unrelated objects on different clocks — the karana on the synodic, the nakshatras on the sidereal.
Lesson 6A.1
The Karana Nobody Wants

Astronomical Chapter 6 defined the tithi as a 12° step of the Moon's elongation from the Sun — thirty of them to a lunar month. The karana is simply the half-step: 6° of elongation, sixty to a lunar month, two to every tithi. It is the fourth limb of the Panchanga and the one most readers skip.

Eleven karanas, in two classes

Classical Eleven karanas exist. Seven are movable (chara) and cycle in a fixed order, repeating eight times over to fill karanas 2 through 57. Four are fixed (sthira), occurring once each per month, bracketing the cycle at either end.

  1. Bava, Balava, Kaulava, Taitila, Gara, Vanija, Vishti — the seven movable karanas, in their unvarying order.
  2. Kimstughna — fixed; always karana 1, opening the lunar month.
  3. Shakuni, Chatushpada, Naga — fixed; karanas 58, 59 and 60, closing it.

Six of the seven movable karanas carry mild flavourings — favourable for this kind of work, less so for that. The seventh does not. Vishti is the one the tradition tells you to stop for.

Vishti and Bhadra are the same thing

Classical The karana is named Vishti; the interval during which it runs is called Bhadra. Muhurta texts and modern almanacs use the two names interchangeably and print the timings under either heading. When a panchang says "Bhadra 12:31 to 23:30," it is telling you when Vishti karana begins and ends — nothing more and nothing less.

Verify Mythologically Bhadra is described as a daughter of Surya and a sister of Shani, fierce-tempered, set by Brahma to obstruct undertakings. This book has not traced the account to a named text and carries it flagged rather than asserted. Little rests on it: the doctrine stands on the muhurta literature, not on the story.

What the prohibition actually covers

Classical Bhadra is a prohibition on beginnings. The standard list of what to avoid runs: marriage, entering a new house, setting out on a journey, coronation or the taking of office, and the inauguration of any venture intended to prosper. Restated for our purposes — the initiation of any transaction whose success you care about.

Verify What it does not cover, in most texts: work already underway, routine maintenance, and — this is the interesting exception — acts of a Saturnine or destructive character. Several sources specifically permit surgery, demolition, litigation and warfare during Bhadra. The exact scope of the exception list varies between texts, and this book will not print it as settled doctrine without a named citation.

Where Bhadra Falls, and Why It Is Arithmetic

Astronomical Because the seven movable karanas repeat in strict order from karana 2 through karana 57, Vishti's position is entirely determined. There is no discretion in it, no astrologer's judgement, nothing to disagree about. Number the karanas 1 to 60 across the lunar month and Vishti lands on exactly these:

The lattice Karana 8, 15, 22, 29, 36, 43, 50 and 57 — eight windows to every lunar month, without exception, in every lunar month there has ever been.

Each of those eight sits on a specific half of a specific tithi, and the pattern is worth learning as a table rather than a rule, because it is the fastest way to sanity-check any almanac you are handed.

The collapse Odd karana number → Day Bhadra. Even karana number → Night Bhadra. Equivalently: the first half of a tithi gives Day Bhadra, the second half gives Night Bhadra. That is the whole classification, and because it is arithmetic it cannot fail — which makes it the cheapest error-detector you will ever run against a published table.

How long a window runs

Astronomical Six degrees of elongation at the mean synodic rate of about 12.19° per day gives roughly 11.8 hours. But the Moon's speed varies with its distance, so the real range is wider. In calendar 2026 the extremes are 10h 06m on 23 December and 13h 20m on 13 December — the shortest and longest windows of the year falling ten days apart, which is what a perigee-to-apogee swing looks like from the inside.

Astronomical A detail that catches almost everyone: fifty-two of the ninety-nine windows in 2026 cross midnight. A reader who checks only today's row in an almanac will routinely miss a window that opened last night and is still running at breakfast.

One rule, two clocks

Callout · Two Clocks, One Karana Bhadra lives entirely on Chapter 6's synodic clock. It is an angular relationship between two bodies, so it is zodiac-free and ayanamsa-free — a Bhadra window is identical in tropical and sidereal calculation, and identical whether you use Lahiri, Raman or KP. The Moon's sign during Bhadra, which Lesson 6A.4 needs, is a placement: sidereal-only and ayanamsa-dependent, on the star clock. One rule, both clocks, kept carefully apart. Confusing them is how practitioners end up arguing about Bhadra timings that were never in dispute.
The rule to lock inBhadra is not a judgement. It is arithmetic on the Moon−Sun angle — 6° wide, eight times a lunar month, computable to the second and identical in every zodiac.

Lesson 6A.3
How to Calculate It — and for Anywhere

Framework Five steps. Any ephemeris that returns Moon and Sun longitudes will do the work; this book uses Swiss Ephemeris throughout.

  1. Longitudes. Compute the tropical longitudes of the Moon and the Sun at the instant in question.
  2. Elongation. Take E = (Moon − Sun) mod 360.
  3. Karana number. K = floor(E ÷ 6) + 1, giving a value from 1 to 60.
  4. Classification. If K is not one of 8, 15, 22, 29, 36, 43, 50, 57, no Bhadra is running. If it is: odd K gives Day Bhadra, even K gives Night Bhadra.
  5. Boundaries. Bisect on the exact 6° multiples — the window opens where E first reaches 6 × (K − 1), and closes where it first reaches 6 × K.

Step five is the one people skip, and it is where the precision lives. Do not sample the elongation on a half-hourly grid and report the grid point as the boundary; that is exactly how a published table ends up two hours out, as Lesson 6A.6 demonstrates on real data.

A window computed in full — 9 September 2026

Astronomical At 00:00 IST the elongation stands at 315.84°, giving karana 53 — Kaulava. Not Bhadra. By 15:00 it has advanced to 324.15°, karana 55 — Gara. Still not Bhadra. Vishti for this lunar month sits at karana 57, which opens at 336°.

Bisecting: the elongation reaches 336.000° at 12:31 IST on 9 September and 342.000° at 23:30 IST the same day. Karana 57 is odd, so this is a Day Bhadra, falling on Krishna Chaturdashi, running 10h 59m. The Moon at the opening instant stands at 118°24' sidereal — Cancer.

Note carefully what the label does not mean. This window runs from lunchtime into late evening, well over half of it after dark, and it is still a Day Bhadra. Lesson 6A.4 explains why, and it is the single most common misreading of the whole doctrine.

Reading a window from another timezone

Framework Three distinct things happen when a Bhadra window is moved to a different part of the world, and keeping them apart is most of the work.

The window itself does not move. Its start and end are absolute instants, defined by the Moon−Sun angle reaching an exact 6° multiple. Converting to another zone changes the digits on the clock and sometimes the calendar date. It changes nothing else, including the duration.

The Day/Night classification does not change either. It derives from karana parity — a property of which half of the tithi you are in. There is no clock and no location anywhere in that derivation. A Day Bhadra is a Day Bhadra in Mumbai, London and New York simultaneously.

Only one thing is local, and it changes with latitude and longitude rather than with the timezone. The crossing rule of Lesson 6A.4 asks whether the window opens after sunrise or after sunset — and sunrise is a local event. Two readers in the same timezone but at different latitudes can legitimately reach different verdicts on the same window.

The 9 September window makes the point concretely. Everything in the upper half of this table is fixed everywhere on Earth; everything in the lower half is not.

A Mumbai-based day/night reading used in another city is a stated approximation, which is a perfectly respectable thing to be. An unstated one is not.

Lesson 6A.4
Reading the Effect on the Day

A Bhadra window becomes something you can act on only after two independent classical tests, and both must fail before the window is genuinely adverse. Most windows fail only one, which is why the doctrine is far less prohibitive in practice than its reputation suggests.

Axis one — the crossing rule

Classical The first test asks whether the window's nature and its timing agree. Bhadra classified as Day but opening after sunset, or classified as Night but opening after sunrise, is held to be defused — the classical formulation being that Day Bhadra is auspicious at night and Night Bhadra auspicious by day. It is the same-period pairing that is live and harmful.

Framework The reference is true sunrise and sunset at the place of the query, evaluated at the moment the window opens. A Bhadra beginning after sunrise is read as "in Day Time"; one beginning after sunset as "in Night Time." Not a fixed clock hour — in 2026 four windows open close enough to a boundary that a fixed 06:00 to 18:00 approximation would classify them differently, which is precisely why the convention must be declared rather than assumed.

The trap, stated plainly "Day Bhadra" is a tithi-half, read off the karana number. "In Day Time" is a clock fact, read off sunrise. They are different questions, and the answer to one tells you nothing about the other. Roughly half of all Day Bhadras open at night. The 9 September window computed in the previous lesson is a Day Bhadra that runs mostly after dark — and in New York it opens at three in the morning and is still a Day Bhadra.

Axis two — Bhadra's address

Classical The second test asks where Bhadra is residing, read from the Moon's sidereal rashi during the window. Only the earthly residence is held to bite.

Framework This axis is sidereal and therefore ayanamsa-dependent, unlike the first — and borderline cases are real. One 2026 window has the Moon at 2°04' into Sagittarius; under Lahiri that is Patala, and a materially different ayanamsa could move it. Flag borderline entries as borderline. Do not launder them into certainty.

The composite verdict

Framework Combining the two axes gives the reading a practitioner actually uses. Both defused gives Positive — a window that may reasonably be ignored. Both live gives Negative — the genuine avoid-window. A split gives Neutral.

Astronomical Computed across calendar 2026: 33 Positive, 50 Neutral, and 16 Negative out of ninety-nine windows. Only about one Bhadra in six is fully live on both axes — a figure worth holding onto when someone describes Bhadra as though it shuts down a fifth of the year.

The five limbs — noted, not reproduced

Verify The tradition carries a refinement this book does not reproduce. The Bhadra interval is subdivided into five limbs — mukha (face), kantha (throat), hridaya (heart), nabhi (navel) and puchha (tail) — with the tail held to be not merely harmless but positively favourable, so that work begun in the closing portion of a Bhadra is said to succeed rather than merely survive.

The proportions assigned to each limb vary between texts, and several sources make them vary by loka as well, so that a Swarga Bhadra and a Mrityu Bhadra are divided differently. Rather than print an averaged figure matching no actual text, this book records that the doctrine exists and leaves the numbers to a properly sourced treatment.

Why the omission is deliberate The temptation here is worth naming. A five-limb subdivision would let a practitioner rescue any window at all by locating the tail — and a rule that can always be satisfied has stopped constraining anything. If the limbs are to be used, the proportions must be fixed in advance from a named text and applied without adjustment, which is exactly what the absence of an agreed set of proportions makes difficult.
The rule to lock inTwo independent tests, and both must fail before a window is genuinely adverse. A table reporting Bhadra as a single yes or no has thrown away most of the doctrine.

Lesson 6A.5
What a Market Practitioner Should Actually Do

Framework Bhadra is a muhurta rule about initiation. Exactly one trading concept maps onto it: entry timing. Not direction, not magnitude, not exits. Everything legitimate follows from that single correspondence, and every abuse follows from ignoring it.

The three legitimate uses

  1. A veto filter on discretionary new entries during windows that are Negative on the composite reading — the sixteen days of 2026, not the ninety-nine windows.
  2. A position-sizing haircut rather than a hard block, for practitioners who prefer a graded response to a binary one.
  3. Scheduling. Where the timing of a planned entry is genuinely yours to choose — a rebalance, a new allocation, the opening of a position with no urgency — choose outside the window. This costs nothing and is the only use that survives every objection in this lesson.

What it cannot do

🧭
1 · Not directional
Never a compass

A Bhadra window contains rallies and sell-offs in whatever proportion the market supplies. Chapter 6's warning applies without modification: this is a clock.

🚪
2 · Nothing about exits
Beginnings only

The prohibition covers initiation. It is silent on holding, adding, trimming and closing — and a rule extended past its own scope is no longer that rule.

📈
3 · Not a signal generator
Eight a month is too many

Eight windows per lunar month is eight fresh reasons to act. Chapter 6's overtrading caution applies with force: the clock should filter attention, never manufacture trades.

The base-rate trap One trading day in five touches a Bhadra window. Any list of bad things that happened during Bhadra will therefore be long by construction — and so will the equivalent list for any other arbitrary 13% slice of the clock. If you find yourself assembling such a list, you are performing Chapter 18's spurious-correlation exercise without noticing. The composite filter is the only genuinely selective one, and eight days a year is far too small a sample to establish anything. A rule this rare needs decades of data before it can be distinguished from noise, and by then the sample is contaminated by every regime change in the period.

The unfalsifiability problem

Framework Practitioners of this material commonly hold that any astrological indication may be neutralised by a contrary indication running at the same time. As classical doctrine that is entirely defensible — competing yogas cancelling is a real principle, and the two-axis structure of Bhadra itself is an instance of it.

As an empirical claim it is a trapdoor. If every failed signal can be attributed to an unnamed simultaneous counter-signal, the rule has been placed permanently beyond testing. That is not a criticism of anyone's sincerity; it is a structural feature of how the claim is worded.

The discipline that repairs it Name the counter-signals in advance. If Bhadra is to be overridden by a specific set of conditions, enumerate them before the test rather than after the result. A rule with a pre-registered exception list is testable. A rule with a retrospective one is not — and the difference between the two is the entire subject of Chapter 19.

Verify There is no peer-reviewed literature on Bhadra and market returns, in either direction. It is untested rather than disproven, and the distinction matters in both directions: nobody has shown it works, and nobody has shown it does not. If you intend to use it, Chapter 19's standard applies without discount — pre-register the rule including its exceptions, backtest across enough lunar months to accumulate a usable sample, and hold out data for genuine out-of-sample testing (Chapter 18).

The rule to lock inBhadra is a veto on beginnings, never a forecast of outcomes. If your rule tells you which way price will go, you have stopped using Bhadra and started using something else.

Lesson 6A.6
Auditing a Bhadra Table — and Its Copies

Framework Bhadra timings are published in thousands of almanacs, apps and annual calendars, and they disagree with one another. Some of that disagreement is legitimate — different reference locations produce different day/night classifications, as Lesson 6A.3 showed. Most of it is not.

The six checks

Run these against any Bhadra table before trusting a single row of it. None requires more than arithmetic, and the first two cannot fail on correctly computed data.

  1. Tithi against karana. Does the tithi label match the karana number? Karana 43 must be Krishna Saptami. Always, with no exceptions.
  2. Day/Night against parity. Odd karana must read Day Bhadra, even must read Night. This is the single cheapest error-detector in the chapter.
  3. Boundaries on the lattice. Do start and end sit on exact 6° elongation multiples?
  4. Sign against ephemeris. Does the Moon's sidereal longitude at the opening instant fall in the printed sign?
  5. Loka against sign. Does the residence follow from the rashi by the table in Lesson 6A.4?
  6. Composite against its inputs. Does the overall verdict follow from the table's own two axis results?

Any table failing check one or check two has been hand-edited somewhere, because neither can fail arithmetically.

The second failure mode, which is the dangerous one

A lesson learned the hard way, in preparing this chapter The audit above was first run against a digital transcription of the printed table rather than the printed pages themselves, and it produced a much longer and much worse error list — including two rows whose Day/Night classification appeared to violate check two, which is arithmetically impossible.

That impossibility was the tell. Checking against the printed pages showed the transcription was corrupt: a single dropped cell had shifted an entire column from mid-December onward, silently misdating four windows, and a second column was scrambled across roughly two dozen rows. Most of the apparent errors were in the copy, not the source. Publishing that first list would have produced an errata that was itself false.

Framework Two distinct failure modes, then, and the second is by far the more dangerous because it is invisible. A computational error in an original is bounded and detectable. A transmission error in a copy makes a correct source look wrong, and it arrives without any of the tells that mark a miscalculation.

Anyone downloading almanac data rather than computing it is exposed to the second mode continuously. Check two — Day/Night against karana parity — is the cheapest available detector for it. It costs nothing, it runs in seconds, and it catches corruption that no amount of careful reading will.

Reproducibility, so you need not take any of this on trust Everything needed to run this audit yourself is in this chapter. 💬 Check yourself · Lesson 6A.6
Q1A table shows karana 50 labelled Day Bhadra. What do you conclude, and why immediately?
AThe table has been hand-edited or corrupted. Karana 50 is even, so it must read Night Bhadra. Check two cannot fail arithmetically, so any failure of it is a human artefact — no further investigation is needed to distrust the row.
Q2What distinguishes a computational error from a transmission error in practice?
AA computational error is bounded and internally consistent — a boundary two hours out, or a sign misread by one. A transmission error breaks impossible things, like parity, and often shifts whole columns, making a correct source appear wrong.
Q3Why does a two-hour offset point to hand editing rather than a different ephemeris?
ABecause ephemerides agree on lunar and solar longitudes to far better than a second of time for a modern date. A discrepancy of exactly two hours, appearing in both directions on isolated rows, is a human or spreadsheet artefact, not a difference of astronomical method.

Chapter 6A — Locked In

You have learned the Panchanga's fifth limb, the one Chapter 6 named and left. Here are the five things to carry forward.

  1. Bhadra is the Vishti karana — a 6° step of Moon−Sun elongation, eight times a lunar month, about 11.8 hours each, sitting on karanas 8, 15, 22, 29, 36, 43, 50 and 57. It is arithmetic, not judgement.
  2. It vetoes beginnings. It is never directional, and it is silent on holding, adding and exiting. A Bhadra rule that predicts direction is no longer a Bhadra rule.
  3. Day and Night Bhadra is a tithi-half, read from karana parity — odd is Day, even is Night. It has nothing to do with daylight and does not change with timezone. Half of all Day Bhadras open after dark.
  4. Two axes, both of which must fail before a window is adverse: the crossing rule against local sunrise, and the loka read from the Moon's rashi. Across 2026 only sixteen windows in ninety-nine are live on both. Only the sunrise test is local.
  5. Compute it yourself. Thirteen of ninety-nine rows in a published 2026 table disagree with the ephemeris — and a corrupt transcription of that same table disagreed far more, in ways that made the original look worse than it was.

The practitioner's checklist

  • 1. Computed to the minute? Never treat Bhadra as a property of a date — the whole value is in knowing which part of the day is clear.
  • 2. Yesterday's window checked? Fifty-two of ninety-nine windows in 2026 cross midnight; today's row alone will not tell you whether one is running now.
  • 3. Day/Night read from the karana number? Never from the clock, and never from the window's position relative to daylight.
  • 4. Sunrise test recomputed for your own coordinates? Mumbai and New York disagree on 84% of windows. If you are using the book's Mumbai basis elsewhere, that is fine — but say so.
  • 5. Both axes checked before treating a window as adverse? Only about one in six is live on both counts; a single-axis reading over-prohibits by a factor of five.
  • 6. Applied to entries only? Never to direction, never to exits, never as a trade trigger in its own right.
  • 7. Exceptions pre-registered? If a contrary indication is allowed to override Bhadra, the list of such indications must be fixed before the test, not assembled after a failure.
  • 8. Six audit checks run on any table you did not compute? Especially check two, which catches transmission corruption that reading will not.

A small glossary — the words you met

Karana
A half-tithi — 6° of Moon−Sun elongation; sixty to a lunar month, and the fifth limb of the Panchanga.
Chara karanas (movable)
Bava, Balava, Kaulava, Taitila, Gara, Vanija, Vishti — the seven that cycle in fixed order, repeating eight times to fill karanas 2 through 57.
Sthira karanas (fixed)
Kimstughna, Shakuni, Chatushpada, Naga — the four occurring once each per lunar month, bracketing the movable cycle at karana 1 and karanas 58 to 60.
Vishti
The seventh movable karana; the only one carrying a prohibition rather than a flavour.
Bhadra
The interval during which Vishti karana runs. Interchangeable with Vishti in almanac usage. Unrelated to the nakshatras Purva and Uttara Bhadrapada.
The karana lattice
Karanas 8, 15, 22, 29, 36, 43, 50 and 57 — the eight fixed positions at which Vishti falls in every lunar month.
Day Bhadra / Night Bhadra
Classification by karana parity — odd gives Day, even gives Night; equivalently, the first versus the second half of a tithi. A property of the lunar month, not of daylight, and invariant with timezone.
The crossing rule
A Bhadra opening in the opposite period to its own classification is held to be defused; the same-period pairing is the live, harmful case.
Bhadra-vaas
Bhadra's residence — which of the three worlds it occupies during a window, read from the Moon's sidereal rashi.
Swarga-loka
The heavenly residence; Moon in Aries, Taurus, Gemini or Scorpio. Held harmless to earthly affairs.
Patala-loka
The nether residence; Moon in Virgo, Libra, Sagittarius or Capricorn. Likewise held harmless here.
Mrityu-loka
The earthly residence; Moon in Cancer, Leo, Aquarius or Pisces. The only case the tradition treats as genuinely malefic.
Mukha, kantha, hridaya, nabhi, puchha
Face, throat, heart, navel and tail — the five limbs into which some texts subdivide a Bhadra interval, the tail being held favourable. Proportions vary by source and are not reproduced in this book.
Muhurta
Electional timing — the choosing of an auspicious moment, assembled from the Panchanga. Bhadra is its most-used prohibition.
