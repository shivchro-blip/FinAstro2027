Lesson 7.1
Why State Decides Everything

A significator names which market force a planet carries (Chapter 4); the sign and house name its costume and arena (Chapters 0 and 5). But none of that tells you whether the force is working well or badly — and that is precisely what state answers. An exalted Jupiter and a debilitated Jupiter are the same significator of expansion and banking; the first reads as robust, well-founded growth, the second as hollow, over-extended optimism. A direct Mercury and a retrograde Mercury are the same significator of trade and information; the first flows, the second stalls and reverses.

This chapter sets out the five dimensions of planetary state — dignity, retrogradation, combustion, stations, and speed — defines each astronomically and classically, and shows how each modulates the interpretation of a significator. One dimension, the station, turns out to be the most directly useful for timing, and feeds straight into the cycle chapters that follow. Throughout, the single discipline holds the line: a strong planet expresses its significations intensely and cleanly — which can sit under a rally or a crash. State changes the quality of a read, never its guaranteed sign.

The rule to lock inCondition, not identity, drives a significator’s expression. The same planet reads robust or hollow, flowing or reversing, by its state — and state changes the quality of a read, never its direction.

Lesson 7.2
The Five Dimensions of Planetary State

1. Dignity — positional strength

Classical A planet’s dignity is its strength by sign position, on a ladder from best to worst.

  1. Exalted (uccha) — maximum strength, at an exact peak degree (Chapter 0): Sun 10° Aries, Moon 3° Taurus, Mars 28° Capricorn, Mercury 15° Virgo, Jupiter 5° Cancer, Venus 27° Pisces, Saturn 20° Libra.
  2. Moolatrikona / own sign (swakshetra) — strong and “at home.”
  3. Friendly sign — comfortable; neutral — workmanlike; enemy sign — weakened.
  4. Debilitated (neecha) — minimum strength, in the sign opposite its exaltation.

Classical Debilitation can be cancelled under certain conditions (Neecha Bhanga), sometimes producing strong results — a real but intricate, author-dependent doctrine, flagged here and not relied upon mechanically.

2. Retrogradation — the strength paradox

Astronomical Retrograde motion is the apparent backward drift of a planet against the stars, an illusion produced by the Earth and the planet moving at different orbital speeds. The Sun and Moon never retrograde; the nodes Rahu and Ketu are always retrograde in mean motion.

Classical Framework Retrogradation carries a genuine paradox a beginner must hold. Vedic strength schemes grant a retrograde planet motional strength (cheshta bala) — it is considered powerful and intense — yet its significations are read as turned inward, abnormal, delayed, or under review. Western practice leans on the second reading: retrograde = revisiting, stalling, reversing. Both are true at once: strong but disrupted.

3. Combustion — burnt by the Sun

Classical A planet too close to the Sun is combust (astangata) — its light, and symbolically its power, is overwhelmed. The classical orbs (degrees from the Sun): Moon 12°, Mars 17°, Mercury 14°, Jupiter 11°, Venus 10°, Saturn 15°. Because Mercury and Venus never stray far from the Sun, they combust frequently. A combust significator is read as weakened, obscured, its theme “burnt.”

4. Stations — the pivot

Astronomical Framework A station is the moment a planet appears to stop as it changes between direct and retrograde motion (stationary-retrograde) or back (stationary-direct). At a station the planet is motionless in longitude and, in both traditions, considered intensely focused and pivotal. For markets this is the most actionable state: the station of a slow planet is watched as a turning-point window — the cycle “hinges” there. Stations feed directly into Chapters 8 and 10.

5. Speed — the tempo

Astronomical Framework Planets move faster or slower than their average at different points in their orbits. Fast motion is read as quick developments; slowing-toward-a-station as stickiness and an impending turn. Speed is the motional component the classical schemes formalize as cheshta bala.

Callout · The Five Questions of State For any planet in a market read, ask: How dignified? (exalted → debilitated) · Direct or retrograde? (flowing vs. under review) · Combust? (burnt by the Sun) · Stationing? (a pivot window) · Fast or slow? (tempo). These five turn “which planet” into “which planet, in what condition.”

Lesson 7.3
How the Tradition Measures Strength

Classical Indian astrology formalizes all of the above into a six-fold strength system, Shadbala (“six strengths”), worth knowing by name even though its full computation is beyond this chapter.

Strength (bala)What it measures
Sthana balapositional strength — dignity, the ladder above
Dig baladirectional strength — a planet strong in its preferred house-direction
Kala balatemporal strength — day/night, paksha, etc. (includes the bright-Moon strength of Chapter 6)
Cheshta balamotional strength — speed and retrogradation
Naisargika balanatural strength — a fixed ranking, Sun strongest down to Saturn
Drik balaaspectual strength — benefic vs. malefic aspects received

Classical The avasthas (planetary “states / moods” — e.g., a planet “asleep,” “awake,” “agitated”) add a further interpretive layer. And the combustion orbs, the exaltation degrees, and the debilitation-cancellation rules above are all part of this same classical machinery.

Framework The lesson to carry forward: the tradition treats strength as a multi-source composite, not a single switch — which is both its sophistication and, as Lesson 7.6 warns, a temptation to false precision.

The rule to lock inThe tradition measures strength as a six-fold composite (Shadbala), not a single switch. Know the six by name — but treat strength as a qualitative blend, never a single decisive number.

Lesson 7.5
Folding State Into the Read

State slots into the reading stack after significator–sign–house:

  1. Assess dignity → set the quality. Is the significator exalted / own (robust), neutral (workmanlike), or debilitated / enemy (hollow)? This calibrates how well the theme is expressed — not its direction.
  2. Check motion → flag disruption / review. Direct = flowing; retrograde = the theme is under review, stalling, or reversing. Note retrograde significators especially (Mercury for trade / data; slow planets for their domains).
  3. Check combustion → discount burnt themes. A significator within the combustion orb of the Sun is weakened; down-weight its reading.
  4. Diarize stations → the turning-point windows. Identify any slow planet at or near a station; mark it as a watch window for a trend hinge, to be confirmed by price structure.
  5. Read speed → gauge tempo. Fast = quick developments; slowing-to-station = stickiness and impending change.
  6. Compose, then test. Combine these into a single quality-and-timing picture for the significator, turn it into a dated hypothesis, and act only on confluence with price, risk fixed first.

Lesson 7.6
Edge Cases and Cautions

A master is defined by how clearly they see the failure modes. Seven cautions guard state-based market work from misuse.

  1. Strength is not a single number. Classical Shadbala yields figures, but dignities conflict (exalted yet combust yet retrograde), Neecha Bhanga can flip a debilitation, and authors weight the six strengths differently. Resist the false precision of “Jupiter scores 1.4, therefore…”; treat strength as a qualitative composite.
  2. The retrograde paradox is real — hold both halves. A retrograde planet is strong (cheshta bala) and disrupted / internalized. It is not simply “good” or “bad.” Beginners who collapse the paradox misread it.
  3. Mercury-retrograde market lore is popular but unsupported. Observed There is no solid evidence that Mercury retrograde moves markets directionally. Use it as a soft disruption / choppiness flag at most; never trade it as a rule.
  4. Combustion orbs vary — and the cazimi exception. Classical Different authors use slightly different combustion orbs. And the Western tradition notes cazimi — a planet within ~17 arc-minutes of the Sun’s exact degree (“in the heart of the Sun”) — which is considered super-strengthened, the opposite of burnt. Near the Sun, distinguish ordinary combustion from the rare cazimi.
  5. “Strong” ≠ “bullish” (the standing correction). Dignity measures intensity and cleanliness of expression, not price direction. An exalted malefic and an exalted benefic are both strong; neither is a buy or sell by itself.
  6. State must be computed, not eyeballed. Exact dignity (degree-sensitive exaltations), combustion (orb-sensitive), and especially station timing require an ephemeris. Station dates in particular shift with the ephemeris and must be looked up precisely (Appendix A).
  7. The standing evidence caveat. Observed State enriches interpretation; it confers no proven predictive power. Its value is in producing better-calibrated, properly-hedged hypotheses — not forecasts.

Chapter 7 — Locked In

You can now read a planet’s condition, not merely its identity — and with it, Part I’s toolkit is complete. Here are the things to carry forward.

  1. Condition, not identity, drives a significator’s market expression. The same planet reads robust or hollow, flowing or reversing, by its state.
  2. Five dimensions of state: dignity (quality), retrogradation (review / disruption — strong yet disrupted), combustion (burnt / weak), stations (turning-point windows), and speed (tempo). The tradition formalizes these as Shadbala.
  3. Stations are the tradeable state — a stationing slow planet dates a potential trend hinge; dignity and combustion only color a read.
  4. Today’s sky is a clean worked example: Jupiter exalted and direct (peak bull / wealth dignity), nothing retrograde but the nodes, nothing combust — read as a robust-expansion backdrop hypothesis, with the caveat that exalted-benefic can equally mark froth.
  5. Strength is not direction, and state is not prediction. It refines the quality and timing of a hypothesis; price structure and fixed risk still decide the trade.

Practitioner’s Checklist — Assessing a Planet’s State

Run all seven gates on any significator before its state reaches your read. A state assessed loosely or eyeballed should never carry weight in a trade.

  • Dignity set? Exalted / own / friendly / neutral / enemy / debilitated — and the quality of expression calibrated (not the direction)?
  • Motion checked? Direct or retrograde? If retrograde, is the theme flagged under review / reversing, with the strength-paradox held?
  • Combustion checked? Within the Sun’s orb (burnt) — or the rare cazimi (super-strong)?
  • Station diarized? Any slow planet at / near a station marked as a turning-point watch window?
  • Speed read? Fast (quick developments) or slowing-to-station (stickiness / turn)?
  • Composed and computed? State assembled qualitatively (no false Shadbala precision) and the degree / orb / station facts actually computed, not eyeballed?
  • Direction from price, risk fixed? “Strong” not mistaken for “bullish”; hypothesis confirmed against structure with independent risk?

A small glossary — the words you met

Dignity
A planet’s strength by sign position, on a ladder from exalted (peak) through own / friendly / neutral / enemy to debilitated (opposite the exaltation sign).
Exalted (uccha)
A planet at its position of maximum strength, peaking at an exact degree — e.g., Jupiter at 5° Cancer.
Debilitated (neecha)
A planet at minimum strength, in the sign opposite its exaltation; can sometimes be cancelled (Neecha Bhanga).
Neecha Bhanga
Debilitation-cancellation — conditions under which a debilitated planet’s weakness is lifted, sometimes producing strong results; intricate and author-dependent.
Retrograde
The apparent backward drift of a planet against the stars, from differing orbital speeds; read as strong (cheshta bala) yet disrupted / under review. The Sun and Moon never retrograde; the nodes always do.
Combustion (astangata)
A planet too close to the Sun, its light and symbolic power overwhelmed; read as weakened and “burnt.” Orbs run from 10° (Venus) to 17° (Mars).
Cazimi
A planet within ~17 arc-minutes of the Sun’s exact degree (“in the heart of the Sun”) — considered super-strengthened, the opposite of combust.
Station
The moment a planet appears to stop as it turns between direct and retrograde motion; motionless in longitude and considered intensely pivotal — the most tradeable state, since it dates a potential turn.
Cheshta bala
Motional strength — the Shadbala component from a planet’s speed and retrogradation.
Shadbala
The six-fold strength system of Indian astrology: sthana, dig, kala, cheshta, naisargika, and drik bala — strength as a multi-source composite, not a single number.
Avasthas
Planetary “states / moods” (e.g., asleep, awake, agitated) — a further interpretive layer in the classical strength machinery.
